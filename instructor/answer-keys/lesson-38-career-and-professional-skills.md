## 28. Answer Key

1. **A.** Observations are traceable facts; analysis explains what those facts may mean.
2. **B.** Executives need consequences, status, decisions, ownership, and timing before technical detail.
3. **C.** Validation and organizational context convert a scanner result into a defensible finding and risk priority.
4. **B.** Public exposure may already have occurred. Revocation or rotation invalidates the credential while investigation proceeds.
5. **C.** Synthetic, reproducible work demonstrates skill without exposing protected information.
6. **B.** A role-based gap analysis prevents certification activity from becoming disconnected from actual work.
7. **C.** It clearly separates confirmed activity from an unresolved investigation question.
8. **A.** It displays the exact staged changes intended for the next commit.
9. **A.** Strong junior analysts write clearly, separate facts from analysis, and state uncertainty accurately.
10. **A.** Portfolio artifacts must remove sensitive data, secrets, real identifiers, and protected organizational details.

Short-answer guidance:

1. Any five of: what happened, affected scope, business impact, actions completed, current status, uncertainty, decisions required, owner, deadline, and next update.
2. Git preserves prior objects and history, and external clones or logs may also retain the value. The secret must be rotated or revoked, use investigated, and history remediated where required.
3. Replace identities and organizations with fictional values, use reserved domains and lab IPs, synthesize logs, recreate screenshots, remove metadata, and review contextual clues.
4. It names the specific action, affected scope, accountable owner, priority or deadline, dependencies, validation criteria, and rollback or risk considerations.
5. Strong answers should use precise confidence language, separate confirmed facts from hypotheses, explain what evidence is missing, state current impact, and describe next investigative steps. Uncertainty should be honest and bounded, not vague or alarmist.

Scenario guidance:

13. State that encryption behavior is confirmed on eight named or categorized systems, describe affected services and immediate business effect, report completed isolation and preservation actions, and say that data exfiltration is not confirmed. Give current investigative actions, recovery readiness, decisions needed, owners, confidence, and the next update time. Do not equate encryption with confirmed data theft.

14. Strong answers should replace real names, domains, IPs, usernames, customer data, screenshots, and metadata with synthetic equivalents; use reserved domains and lab IP ranges; remove secrets; preserve the learning objective; document scope, evidence, methods, limitations, and conclusions; and avoid implying access to real employer or customer systems. The artifact should demonstrate reasoning, not expose sensitive information.
