# Instructor Answer Key - Lesson 37: Compliance Frameworks

Do not distribute this file before the assessment closes. Accept defensible scenario and theory answers when students support their reasoning with accurate security concepts, evidence, and business context.

## 23. Answer Key

1. **A.** Govern addresses organizational context, strategy, policy, roles, oversight, and cybersecurity supply-chain risk.
2. **C.** PCI DSS is an industry security standard whose obligations commonly arise through participation and contractual payment relationships.
3. **B.** The Statement of Applicability records necessary controls, implementation status, and justification for inclusion or exclusion.
4. **C.** Addressable requires documented assessment and a reasonable, appropriate decision; it is not automatic permission to omit a safeguard.
5. **C.** Complete population and action evidence demonstrates that the review occurred and that identified access was corrected.
6. **B.** Requiring behavior is a directive function. The policy itself is managerial by category, while the reporting procedure may be operational.
7. **B.** Similar controls do not erase differences in applicability, wording, scope, objectives, or required proof.
8. **A.** Data minimization limits personal data to what is adequate, relevant, and necessary for the stated purpose.
9. **A.** CIS IG1 is the basic cyber hygiene starting point for smaller or less mature organizations.
10. **A.** Audit evidence is information used to evaluate whether requirements or controls are satisfied.

Short-answer guidance:

1. Design effectiveness asks whether a properly performed control can meet its objective. Operating effectiveness asks whether it actually operated consistently, across the correct population and period, with exceptions resolved.
2. The provider and customer have different scopes and responsibilities. The customer must configure the service, govern identities and data, verify contractual terms, monitor use, and confirm that the certified scope covers the relevant service.
3. Any six of: requirement, affected scope, reason, risk scenario, residual risk, compensating control, owner, approver, remediation, expiry, review date, monitoring, or evidence.
4. A SOC analyst operates and evidences monitoring, investigates events, documents actions, preserves logs, and escalates facts. Legal, privacy, compliance, and accountable leaders determine applicability, notification, and formal risk acceptance.
5. Scope defines what the compliance claim actually covers. It must include relevant people, processes, systems, data, suppliers, locations, and evidence because excluding a dependency or data flow can create false assurance.

Scenario guidance:

13. Excluding contractors is a design or scoping defect because the procedure omits part of the privileged population. Failure to disable departed administrators is an operating failure and active security exposure. Validate and disable access under approved incident/change procedures, preserve identity and activity logs, determine whether the accounts were used, notify IAM and incident-response owners, and review connected credentials. Reconcile HR/vendor sources against the identity population, correct the procedure, automate joiner-mover-leaver triggers, reperform the full review, validate removals, document the finding, and monitor corrective effectiveness.

14. Strong answers should explain that provider certifications do not automatically cover the clinic's configuration, users, data flows, contracts, incident procedures, payment scope, or evidence obligations. The clinic should identify applicable HIPAA, PCI, privacy, contractual, and internal policy obligations; map shared responsibilities; review SaaS and payment-provider scope; enable MFA and audit logging; verify data protection and retention; collect vendor attestations; document decisions; and assign owners for monitoring, access review, incident response, and exceptions.
